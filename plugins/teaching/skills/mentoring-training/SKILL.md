# Mentoring & Training Skill

A comprehensive skill for supporting mentors, coaches, managers, L&D professionals, and individuals who want to develop themselves or others — with AI skills and mindset embedded throughout every phase of the training lifecycle.

## AI Integration Principles (Apply Everywhere)

AI is not a separate topic — it is woven into every coaching plan, training module, assignment, and reflection. Four dimensions apply across all outputs:

| Dimension | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| 🤖 **AI Tool Use** | Suggest specific AI tools (Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, Midjourney, etc.) relevant to the task at each milestone |
| 🧠 **Critical Thinking** | Include prompts that ask the learner to evaluate AI outputs — when to trust, verify, or override them |
| ✍️ **Prompt Engineering** | Teach learners to write better prompts as a skill, not an afterthought |
| ⚖️ **AI Ethics** | Surface ethical considerations (bias, privacy, attribution, over-reliance) naturally within tasks |

**For every milestone, training module, and assignment, always add an "AI Angle" section** covering:
- Which AI tools are relevant here and how to use them
- A "try with AI / try without AI" comparison exercise
- A reflection prompt: *"Where did AI help? Where did it fall short or mislead you?"*

## Coaching & Mentoring

### Step 1: Understand the Context
Before creating a plan, gather:
- **Who is being coached?** (role, experience level, industry)
- **What's the goal?** (skill gap, career transition, performance improvement, leadership development)
- **Timeframe?** (weeks, months, ongoing)
- **What's already been tried?**

If the user doesn't provide this, ask 2–3 targeted questions. Don't ask everything at once.

### Step 2: Build a Personalized Coaching Plan
Structure:

```
COACHING PLAN: [Name/Role]
Duration: [X weeks/months]
Goal: [Primary development objective]

CURRENT STATE
- Strengths: ...
- Development areas: ...
- Blockers / challenges: ...

MILESTONES
For each milestone, always include:
- Focus area + actions
- Learning outcomes: what the mentee will KNOW, be able to DO, and demonstrate by end of period
- How to assess: what "done" looks like (observable evidence)

Example format:
Month 1: [Focus Area]
  Actions: ...
  Learning Outcomes:
    - KNOW: [Concept or framework they understand]
    - DO: [Skill they can perform independently]
    - SHOW: [Tangible deliverable or observable behavior that proves it]
  AI Angle:
    - Tool: [Specific AI tool relevant to this milestone and how to use it]
    - Assignment: Try [task] with AI assistance, then repeat a version without. Compare results.
    - Reflection: Where did AI accelerate your work? Where did it produce something generic, wrong, or that needed your human judgment to fix?
    - Ethics check: [Any relevant consideration — e.g. data privacy, attribution, over-reliance]
...

SESSION STRUCTURE (per meeting)
- Check-in (5 min): How are they doing?
- Progress review (10 min): Last week's commitments
- Deep dive (30 min): Core topic / challenge
- Forward planning (10 min): Next steps + accountability
- Reflection (5 min): What did we learn?

ACCOUNTABILITY MECHANISMS
- Weekly check-ins / journaling prompts
- Stretch assignments
- Recommended reading / resources

SUCCESS METRICS
- [Measurable outcome 1]
- [Measurable outcome 2]
```

### Step 3: Offer Coaching Conversation Frameworks
Suggest frameworks appropriate to the situation:
- **GROW model** (Goal, Reality, Options, Will) — for goal-setting sessions
- **SBI** (Situation, Behavior, Impact) — for feedback conversations
- **5 Whys** — for root cause exploration
- **Appreciative Inquiry** — for strengths-based development

## Training Materials

### Step 1: Define the Training
Clarify:
- **Topic / skill to be taught**
- **Audience** (role, existing knowledge level)
- **Format** (self-paced doc, slide deck, workshop, video script)
- **Length / depth** (overview vs. deep dive)

### Step 2: Design the Curriculum
Use a standard instructional design structure:

```
MODULE: [Title]
Learning Objectives: By the end, learners will be able to...
  1. [Objective 1 — use action verbs: explain, demonstrate, apply]
  2. [Objective 2]

CONTENT OUTLINE
  1. Introduction / Why This Matters (hook the learner)
  2. Core Concept A
     - Explanation
     - Example
     - Common mistake to avoid
  3. Core Concept B
     ...
  4. Practical Application / Case Study
  5. Summary & Key Takeaways
  6. Next Steps / Further Resources

DELIVERY NOTES
  - Estimated time: X minutes
  - Suggested activities: [discussion, role play, demo, etc.]
  - Materials needed: [handouts, tools, etc.]
```

### Step 3: Write the Content
Produce the actual training content following the outline. Use:
- Plain language (avoid jargon unless audience-appropriate)
- Concrete examples and scenarios
- "You will…" framing to speak directly to the learner
- Callout boxes for key tips, warnings, and definitions

### Step 4: Embed AI Into Every Module
For every training module produced, always add an **"AI in Practice" section** at the end:

```
AI IN PRACTICE — [Module Topic]

🤖 Try this with AI:
  [Specific prompt the learner can paste into Claude/ChatGPT to explore this topic]
  Example: "Act as a UX researcher. Help me write a 5-question discussion guide for
  users of a food delivery app who are over 50."

🧠 Then ask yourself:
  - What did the AI get right? What was generic or wrong?
  - What context did you need to add that the AI didn't know?
  - Would you send this output to a stakeholder without editing? Why / why not?

✍️ Prompt tip:
  [One concrete prompt engineering tip relevant to this topic]
  Example: "Add 'Be specific and avoid generic UX advice' to any research prompt
  to get more targeted output."

⚖️ Ethics consideration:
  [One relevant ethical issue to be aware of]
  Example: "If you use AI to synthesise interview notes, be careful not to paste
  in personally identifiable participant information."
```

## Assessments & Quizzes

### Step 1: Clarify Scope
- What topic / module is being assessed?
- What level of difficulty? (beginner / intermediate / advanced)
- Question types? (multiple choice, true/false, short answer, scenario-based)
- How many questions?
- Is there a passing score / rubric needed?

### Step 2: Generate the Assessment

For **multiple choice**, use this format:
```
Q[N]. [Question text]
A) [Option]
B) [Option]
C) [Option]
D) [Option]
✓ Correct: [Letter] — [Brief explanation of why]
```

For **scenario-based questions**:
```
Q[N]. [Scenario description — 2–4 sentences setting context]
What should [character] do next?
A) ...
B) ...
✓ Correct: [Letter] — [Explanation linking back to learning objective]
```

For **short answer / reflection**:
```
Q[N]. [Open-ended question]
Sample strong answer: [2–4 sentence model response]
What to look for: [Key elements evaluator should check]
```

### Step 3: Provide an Answer Key & Rubric
Always include:
- Answer key with explanations
- Scoring guide (e.g., 80%+ = pass)
- Rubric for any subjective questions

### Step 4: Add an AI-Assisted Assessment Task
Always append one open-ended AI task to every assessment:

```
BONUS — AI CRITICAL THINKING TASK
Use an AI tool of your choice to answer [core question from this assessment].
Then write 3–5 sentences evaluating the response:
  - What was accurate?
  - What was missing, wrong, or oversimplified?
  - What prompt change would have improved the output?

This is not graded on whether the AI was right — it's graded on the quality
of your critical evaluation.
```

## Feedback & Performance Reviews

### Step 1: Understand the Situation
- Who is the feedback for? (peer, direct report, manager)
- What triggered this? (regular review, specific incident, request)
- What tone is appropriate? (developmental, corrective, celebratory)
- Are there specific behaviors or outcomes to address?

### Step 2: Apply the SBI Framework
Structure all feedback using **Situation → Behavior → Impact**:

```
SITUATION: When [specific time/context]...
BEHAVIOR: I observed [specific, observable action — not character judgment]...
IMPACT: The effect was [outcome on team / project / stakeholder]...
NEXT STEP: Going forward, I'd like to see [specific alternative behavior / ask].
```

### Step 3: Write the Review or Feedback Document

**For performance reviews**, include:
```
PERFORMANCE REVIEW: [Name] | [Period]

OVERALL RATING: [Exceeds / Meets / Developing / Below Expectations]

STRENGTHS
[2–3 specific, evidenced strengths with examples]

AREAS FOR DEVELOPMENT
[1–2 specific, actionable development areas with examples]

GOALS FOR NEXT PERIOD
1. [SMART goal]
2. [SMART goal]

MANAGER COMMENTS
[Personalized narrative — warm, honest, forward-looking]
```

**For quick feedback**, use a 3-part structure:
- What went well (specific)
- What could be stronger (specific + constructive)
- What to try next time (actionable)

### Tone Guidelines
- Use **"I" statements**, not "you always/never"
- Focus on **behaviors**, not personality
- Be **specific** — vague feedback isn't actionable
- Balance challenge with encouragement
- For corrective feedback: be direct, kind, and clear about expectations

### AI Angle for Feedback
When writing or coaching feedback, suggest the mentor/manager:
- Use AI to **draft** a first version of feedback, then humanise it with specific examples only they know
- Never send AI-generated feedback unedited — it lacks the personal context that makes feedback land
- Reflection prompt for the reviewee: *"Did any part of this feedback feel generic? If so, ask your manager for a specific example."*
- Ethics note: Do not paste private performance data or personal details into public AI tools without checking your organisation's data policy.

## Group Learning & Workshops

### Step 1: Workshop Design
Clarify:
- Topic and learning objectives
- Group size and audience
- Duration (1 hour / half-day / full-day)
- Desired style (lecture, discussion, hands-on, hybrid)

### Step 2: Build the Run-of-Show

```
WORKSHOP: [Title]
Duration: [X hours]
Audience: [X participants, role/level]
Objectives: By the end, participants will...

RUN-OF-SHOW
[00:00] Welcome & Icebreaker (10 min)
  - Activity: [e.g., "Share your name + one word for how you feel about [topic]"]

[00:10] Context Setting (15 min)
  - Why does this matter? (hook)
  - Agenda overview

[00:25] Core Content Block 1 (20 min)
  - [Topic]
  - Delivery: [lecture / video / demo]

[00:45] Activity / Discussion (15 min)
  - [Prompt or exercise]
  - Debrief: [Key questions to ask the group]

[01:00] Core Content Block 2 (20 min)
  ...

[01:45] Synthesis & Action Planning (10 min)
  - Key takeaways
  - Each participant commits to 1 action

[01:55] Q&A + Close (5 min)
```

### Step 3: Facilitation Tips
Include context-specific facilitation guidance:
- How to manage dominant vs. quiet participants
- What to do if the discussion goes off track
- How to handle disagreement productively
- Suggested energy-check moments for longer sessions

### Step 4: Build in an AI Live Exercise
Every workshop should include at least one **live AI exercise** (10–15 min):

```
LIVE AI EXERCISE
Task: [Give participants a realistic task relevant to the workshop topic]
Step 1: Each person writes their own answer first (5 min)
Step 2: Everyone pastes the same prompt into an AI tool (2 min)
Step 3: Small group debrief (5 min):
  - How did the AI response compare to yours?
  - What did it miss that you included?
  - How would you improve the prompt?
  - Would you trust this output in a real work situation?

Facilitator note: The goal is not "AI is good or bad" — it's building the habit
of critical evaluation and intentional use.
```

## Quality Principles (Apply to All Outputs)

1. **Be specific** — generic advice is rarely useful. Use real examples wherever possible.
2. **Match the audience** — calibrate language, depth, and tone to who is being trained or reviewed.
3. **Make it actionable** — every output should have clear next steps.
4. **Be honest and kind** — especially for feedback. Avoid sugarcoating or harshness.
5. **Measure what matters** — include success criteria or metrics wherever appropriate.
6. **Ask before assuming** — if key context is missing, ask 1–2 targeted questions before producing output.
7. **AI is always present** — every output must include an AI Angle: a relevant tool suggestion, a try-with/without exercise, a reflection prompt, and an ethics consideration. Never produce a coaching plan, training module, assessment, or workshop without it.
